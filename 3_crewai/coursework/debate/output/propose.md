I strongly support the motion: **LLMs must be given access to all enterprise data to get more value**.

Why? Because **value comes from context**. An LLM without access to the full enterprise picture is like a brilliant employee locked out of most of the company’s knowledge. It can answer generic questions, but it cannot reliably solve real business problems. The biggest gains from LLMs come when they can connect the dots across emails, documents, customer records, contracts, support tickets, financial data, and internal policies. That is where true productivity, better decisions, and automation live.

If you restrict access, you force the model to work with incomplete information. That leads to shallow answers, missed connections, duplicated work, and lower-quality outputs. But when the model can see the relevant data across the enterprise, it can:
- give more accurate and tailored responses,
- surface hidden insights,
- automate complex workflows,
- reduce time spent searching for information,
- and help employees make faster, better decisions.

This is not just about convenience. It is about **competitive advantage**. Enterprises that unlock their full data for AI will move faster than those that keep their information fragmented. They will identify risks earlier, serve customers better, and turn internal knowledge into a scalable asset.

Yes, access must be governed. “All data” does not mean uncontrolled exposure to everyone; it means the LLM should be capable of accessing the full data landscape **with proper permissions, security, and audit controls**. That is the only way to maximize value while protecting sensitive information.

In short, **an LLM is only as valuable as the context it can use**. Limit the data, and you limit the intelligence. Give it access to the enterprise knowledge base, and you transform it from a chat tool into a genuine business engine.

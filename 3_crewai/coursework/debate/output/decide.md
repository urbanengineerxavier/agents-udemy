The **opposition is more convincing**.

### Why
The pro side makes a strong case that LLMs become more useful when they have access to more context, and that enterprise value often comes from connecting information across systems. That is persuasive in principle. However, the motion is not merely that LLMs should have access to *relevant* enterprise data or *well-governed* enterprise data. It says they **must be given access to all enterprise data** to get more value.

That is where the pro case overreaches.

The opposition wins on the key question of necessity. They argue, convincingly, that:

- **value comes from relevant access, not total access**,
- **most enterprise data is not needed for most tasks**,
- **blanket access creates security, privacy, and compliance risk**,
- and **better retrieval and permissioning** can deliver the needed value without exposing everything.

This is a strong rebuttal because it attacks the motion directly: access to *all* enterprise data is not required to unlock value. In fact, granting that level of access may reduce value by introducing risk, noise, and distrust.

### The strongest point
The most persuasive argument is the least-privilege principle. Enterprises are built around controlled access for a reason. If an LLM can perform well with scoped access to the data relevant to the task, then the motion’s claim of “must” is too absolute. The opposition successfully shows that more access is not automatically more value.

### Conclusion
The pro side is right that **more context can improve usefulness**, but the opposition is more convincing overall because it shows that **full access to all enterprise data is neither necessary nor prudent**. Therefore, the motion is not supported as stated.

**Decision: Oppose the motion.**

I oppose the motion.

**LLMs do not need access to all enterprise data to create more value — they need access to the right data, under strict control.**  
Giving a model blanket access to everything is not intelligence; it is exposure. The more data you feed into an LLM, the more you increase risk without guaranteeing better outcomes.

Here’s the core problem: **value does not come from total access; it comes from relevant access.** Most enterprise data is irrelevant to most tasks. If an LLM can only perform well when it sees everything, that is a design weakness, not a business necessity. The best enterprise systems are built on **least-privilege access**, not maximum exposure. That principle exists for a reason: confidentiality, compliance, and safety.

There are three major reasons this motion fails:

1. **Security and privacy risk**
   Enterprise data includes trade secrets, employee records, customer information, legal materials, and financials. Giving an LLM broad access creates a dangerous attack surface. Even with safeguards, more access means more chance of leakage, misuse, or accidental disclosure. A tool that can summarize data can also expose it.

2. **More data does not automatically mean better value**
   LLMs are not magic. They can be distracted by noise, overwhelmed by conflicting sources, or produce confident but wrong answers when context is too broad. The goal is not to let the model “see everything”; the goal is to let it retrieve the **minimum necessary information** to answer correctly. Better retrieval, not total access, is what drives value.

3. **Enterprise value depends on governance, not hoarding**
   Real business value comes from trustworthy systems. If employees fear that every internal document is accessible to an AI, adoption drops. If legal and compliance teams cannot control what is exposed, the project stalls. A narrow, permissioned, auditable system can deliver strong results without endangering the organization.

So the better approach is simple: **give LLMs scoped, role-based, purpose-specific access to approved data sources**. That maximizes usefulness while minimizing risk. Enterprises should build AI that is useful by design, not reckless by default.

**In short: access to all enterprise data is not required for value — and insisting on it would trade safety for a false promise of intelligence.**
